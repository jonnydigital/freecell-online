In Draw 1 Klondike, you flip one card at a time from the stock. If it plays somewhere, you choose whether to use it. If it doesn't, you draw again. There is no limit on passes, so you can cycle through the whole deck as many times as you need.

That sounds forgiving. But there is a catch: the order of the stock never changes between passes unless you change the tableau. The card you skipped on pass 1 will be in exactly the same position on pass 2. The only thing that changes what you see is what you do to the tableau in between.

This is the core fact of Draw 1 cycling, and ignoring it is what turns unlimited passes into a slow walk toward a dead game.

## Key Takeaways

- The stock order is fixed between passes. The only thing that changes what plays is changes to the tableau.
- Use your first pass to play obvious cards and notice which ones you couldn't play and why.
- Before each new pass, ask what tableau change would unblock the cards you missed.
- Three full passes with no new plays is a strong signal the game is done.

## How Draw 1 Cycling Works

When you run out of stock cards, the waste pile flips back into the stock in the same order it came out. The first card you drew is still first. The card you skipped is still in the same relative position.

Nothing resets. Nothing shuffles. The only way to change what plays from the stock is to change the tableau so that a card you couldn't place before now has somewhere to go.

This has a direct implication: if you cycle through the whole deck without making any tableau moves, your second pass is identical to your first. You will see the same cards in the same order, and none of them will play to the tableau for the same reasons they didn't play the first time.

Every useful second pass starts with a change in the tableau.

## What to Do on Your First Pass

Play the obvious cards. If the stock card plays to a tableau sequence and the play is clearly useful, make it. If the stock card plays to the foundation and it is safe to send up, send it.

But notice what you couldn't play and think about why. This is the useful part of pass 1: you are building a mental map of the stock.

If a 7 of hearts comes up and there is nowhere for it to go, ask: where would it go if the tableau were different? Maybe the only open slot is under a 5 of clubs that you can't move yet. Now you know that moving the 5 somewhere would unlock the 7 on your next pass.

You don't need to act on that information immediately. You just need to hold it long enough to make one tableau move that changes something.

## How to Use Your Second Pass

By the start of your second pass, you should have a specific list of stock cards that didn't play and a rough idea of what would have to change for each one.

On pass 2, check whether your tableau moves opened any of those slots. If the blocking card from the example above is gone, the card you were waiting for should now play. If you didn't move it, it still won't.

That feedback loop is how Draw 1 cycling improves a position. You don't cycle hoping for luck. You cycle with a specific hypothesis, test it, and use the result to plan the next round of tableau moves.

The [Klondike cheat sheet](/klondike-cheat-sheet) lists the priority order for tableau moves. When you are stuck on what to change before pass 2, use that order to rank your options.

## Pass 3 and Beyond: What It Means

If you reach pass 3 with nothing new playing, something is wrong. Either the tableau changes you made didn't address the actual block, or the position has no more solutions in this direction.

Pass 3 is the time to look harder. Check for indirect routes: can you move card A onto card B, which lets card C move to card D, which finally unblocks the stock card you need? Chains of three or four moves sometimes unlock stuck positions that look sealed.

If pass 3 comes and goes with nothing useful, and pass 4 looks the same, the game is likely dead or very close to it. The [Klondike dead game detection guide](/blog/klondike-dead-game-detection) covers how to read a stuck position and decide whether to keep trying or start fresh.

## The Tableau-First Rule

When you have a choice between making a tableau move and drawing from the stock, make the tableau move first.

This sounds obvious, but it is easy to fall into a habit of drawing from the stock out of habit. Every draw that is not preceded by a useful tableau change either confirms a card still does not play, which you already knew, or uses up a position in the cycle without gaining anything.

The rule: before you draw, ask whether any tableau move might change what plays next. If yes, make that move first. If no, then draw.

This is the same logic the [Klondike stock cycle trap guide](/blog/klondike-stock-cycle-trap) uses as a diagnostic tool. Applying it before you get stuck prevents the trap from forming in the first place.

## Tracking Stock Dependencies Without Notes

Most players cycle Draw 1 by feel. With practice, you can carry a rough mental model of the stock without writing anything down.

The method is to note one or two waiting cards per pass: the cards that came up but couldn't play and that you are actively trying to unblock. Don't try to track the entire deck. Track the two or three cards that matter most.

When your waiting cards finally play, add the next ones to your list. This keeps the cognitive load manageable while still giving you something to aim for between passes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many passes through the stock is normal in Draw 1 Klondike?

Most winnable games are resolved within four to six passes. Some positions require more if the tableau has many buried cards. If you have made six or more passes with only a few new plays per cycle, the game is probably near its end.

### Should I always play a stock card if I can?

No. Sometimes it is better to skip a stock card if playing it now would leave a worse tableau state than waiting. For example, if a stock 6 could go on a 7, but the 7 is already in a useful spot and moving the 6 there would strand it, skipping the play may be the right call.

### Does it matter which card I play when I have multiple legal options?

Yes. When you have both a tableau move and a stock play available, think about which one changes the board more usefully. Usually tableau moves that reveal face-down cards come before stock plays that only add to an existing sequence. The [Klondike opening moves guide](/blog/klondike-opening-moves) shows how this priority is applied from the start of the game.

### What if I can't remember which stock cards didn't play?

Focus on one or two key cards rather than the whole deck. If you can remember the rank and suit of the most important blocked card, that is usually enough to guide your tableau decisions before the next pass.

### How is Draw 1 cycling different from Draw 3?

In Draw 3, you see only every third card per pass, so cycling strategy centers on phase management and which passes give access to which cards. Draw 1 shows you every card on every pass, so the challenge is purely about what the tableau allows, not what is visible.
