Pick up Klondike Solitaire for the first time and you will be asked one question before the game even starts: Draw 1 or Draw 3? The choice is not cosmetic. These are two substantially different games sharing the same card layout. Each one rewards different skills, produces different win rates, and requires different habits to play well.

## Key Takeaways

- Draw 1 reveals one card from the stock per click. Draw 3 reveals three, but you can only play the top one each pass.
- Draw 1 gives you complete stock access on every pass. Draw 3 restricts you to seeing one-third of the stock per cycle.
- Win rates are meaningfully different: Draw 1 games are solvable at a much higher rate than Draw 3.
- Beginners learn better on Draw 1. The cycle math in Draw 3 adds overhead that distracts from learning the tableau basics.
- Vegas scoring is almost always paired with Draw 3, which is why many players associate Klondike with the harder mode.

## How the Two Modes Actually Work

In Draw 1, you click the stock and one card appears face-up in the waste pile. You can play it immediately or leave it. When the stock runs out, you flip the waste pile and cycle through again.

In Draw 3, clicking the stock reveals a fan of three cards. Only the top card is available to play. When you play that card, the next one in the fan becomes available. When the stock empties, you flip the waste and cycle again.

The critical difference: Draw 3 gives you access to roughly one-third of the stock per cycle. If there are 24 cards in the stock, you can reach eight of them on each pass through. To reach the other sixteen, you need to clear enough waste cards first. That ordering dependency is the entire game in Draw 3.

## Why Draw 1 Teaches Better Habits

Draw 1 removes the cycle math and lets you focus on what matters in Klondike: reading the tableau, sequencing moves, and deciding when to send a card to the foundation.

In Draw 1, if a card you need is in the stock, you will eventually reach it. Patience is enough. The challenge is making good decisions in the tableau before you cycle through.

This is a cleaner learning environment. You can run bad sequences, notice the consequence, and understand why the move was wrong, without also having to track position offsets in the waste pile. [Klondike for beginners](/klondike-for-beginners) recommends Draw 1 precisely for this reason: the tableau logic is the hard part, and Draw 3 adds noise before you have the core down.

## The Hidden Math in Draw 3

Draw 3 introduces ordering constraints that completely change how you plan moves.

When you cycle through the stock, every card you play shifts the phase of what you will see next time. Play one card from a group of three, and the next cycle shows you a different card in that position. Play two, and the shift is different again.

This means decisions in Draw 3 are not just about what you can play right now. They are about what you will be able to reach in the next cycle, and the one after that.

The [Draw 3 cycle math guide](/blog/klondike-draw-3-cycle-math) walks through this in detail. The short version: a card you cannot play today might be accessible in two cycles if you build the right sequence now, or it might stay buried forever if you make the wrong play first.

Strong Draw 3 players maintain a rough mental count of where key cards sit in the waste pile. They plan moves specifically to shift the cycle so a buried card surfaces in a useful position. This is a real skill, and it takes time to develop.

## Win Rates: What the Numbers Say

Exact win rates depend on how the player is defined. Played perfectly by a solver, Draw 1 Klondike has a solvability rate somewhere in the range of 80 percent, and optimal-play win rates in practical games are meaningfully higher than Draw 3.

Draw 3 is significantly harder. Many Draw 3 game states have no winning line even with perfect play, and the structural bottleneck is almost always the cycle math: a card you need is in the wrong position in the stock and can never be reached without the right predecessor plays.

For [Klondike probability](/klondike-probability) details and what solvability actually means in practice, the breakdown is worth reading separately. The takeaway here: Draw 1 is winnable far more often, and the difficulty gap between the modes is larger than it looks from just one game.

## Vegas Scoring and Draw 3

If you have ever seen Klondike described as the game that costs $52 to start and pays $5 per card moved to the foundation, that is Vegas scoring. And Vegas scoring is almost always paired with Draw 3.

The pairing makes sense for gambling purposes: Draw 3 keeps win rates low enough that the house edge is substantial. A player averaging 20 to 25 cards to the foundation per game is losing money consistently in Vegas scoring.

[Vegas Klondike scoring explained](/klondike-vegas-scoring) covers the math in full, including how the $5-per-card payout structure was designed to be just plausible enough to keep players engaged. The short version: unless you are playing casually, the long-run math of Vegas scoring on Draw 3 is designed to favor the house.

## When to Play Draw 1

Draw 1 is the right choice if:

- You are new to Klondike and want to learn how the game actually works.
- You are playing casually and want more wins per hour.
- You want to practice tableau technique without cycle math as a second variable.
- You are playing a timed game where seeing cards faster matters.

Draw 1 is not "easy mode" in a dismissive sense. Playing Draw 1 optimally still requires good sequencing, good foundation timing, and good judgment about spaces. It is a complete game. It is just not the same game as Draw 3.

## When to Play Draw 3

Draw 3 is the right choice if:

- You are comfortable with Draw 1 and want a harder challenge.
- You want to develop the cycle-counting skill that separates intermediate players from strong ones.
- You are playing Vegas scoring.
- You are looking for a game where perfect play produces a noticeably different outcome than average play.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Draw 1 or Draw 3 more common online?

It depends on the platform. Casual sites and phone apps tend to default to Draw 1. Sites that cater to experienced players often default to Draw 3. Neither is the "official" mode; both are standard Klondike rules.

### Can I win Draw 3 without counting cycles?

Yes, but your win rate will be lower than it should be. Many Draw 3 games are winnable only if you reach specific stock cards in a specific order. Without tracking position, you will occasionally play yourself into dead ends that were avoidable.

### Is it cheating to use undo in Draw 3?

No, undo is a standard tool in most digital implementations and does not change whether the game was fundamentally solvable. It does make it easier to recover from cycle-math errors. For learning, using undo freely is useful. For tracking your "true" win rate, some players prefer one pass without undo.

### Does the number of stock cycles matter in Draw 1?

Less than in Draw 3, but yes. In Draw 1, every card surfaces once per cycle. Planning moves to avoid burying a needed card is the same concept, just simpler: if a card is in the waste pile, you know exactly when you will reach it next. In Draw 3, the same calculation involves modular arithmetic across the fan positions.

### What is a good win rate for Draw 3 with undo?

With unlimited undo and good technique, experienced players report win rates in the range of 30 to 50 percent. Some games are unwinnable regardless of play; the rest depend on cycle management. If you are winning more than half your Draw 3 games with undo, you are playing at a strong level.
