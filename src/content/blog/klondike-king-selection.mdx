Opening an empty column in Klondike is a real milestone. Only a King can fill it, so the moment you create one, you face a decision that shapes the rest of the game: which King goes there, and when?

Most players fill the space quickly, often with the first King they see. That reflex costs more wins than any other single habit.

## Key Takeaways

- An empty column is one of the most valuable assets on the Klondike board. Filling it with the wrong King, or too early, wastes it.
- The best King to play is the one that creates the longest, most useful build based on the cards currently available on the tableau and in the stock.
- A King with a usable Queen already visible is almost always better than a King whose Queen is buried.
- If no King improves your position, leaving the column empty is a valid choice for several turns.
- The [Klondike cheat sheet](/klondike-cheat-sheet) has a quick-reference column for King priorities at different game stages.

## Why King Selection Matters More Than It Looks

An empty column in Klondike does two things at once. It gives you a place to put a King, and it gives you a temporary parking space for any card while you rearrange sequences. The moment you fill it with a King, you lose the second benefit.

Before placing any King, ask: is there a rearrangement I could make right now that needs an empty column as a relay? If yes, do that first. The empty column is worth more as open space than as a King landing spot until you have exhausted those moves.

The [empty tableau spaces guide](/blog/klondike-tableau-spaces) covers the broader value of open columns. King selection is a downstream decision from that framework, not a separate one.

## How to Rank Your Kings When You Have a Choice

When you have multiple Kings available, rank them by how much useful work each one can support. Walk through these questions in order:

**Is there a visible Queen of the right color?** A King needs a Queen of the opposite color to start building. If the Hearts King is available and there is a black Queen sitting face-up and unplaced on the tableau, that combination is immediately productive. A King with no accessible Queen just occupies space.

**How many visible cards can extend the sequence?** A King with an accessible Queen, Jack, 10, 9 already in view is worth more than a King with only a Queen in sight. The more of the initial build you can complete right away, the more reveal opportunities you create.

**Does placing this King expose or free anything?** Sometimes a King is sitting on top of a face-down card you want to uncover. Moving that King to the empty column lets you flip the buried card while starting a useful sequence. That is the best-case King placement: it creates reveals in two locations at once.

**What suit does the King represent?** If Hearts and Diamonds are both moving quickly toward the foundation, a red King probably starts a more productive sequence than a black one. Suit-building momentum from earlier in the game matters here.

The [suit priority guide](/blog/klondike-suit-priority) explains how to track which suits are ahead and which are blocked. That read feeds directly into which King is worth placing now.

## When to Leave the Column Empty Instead

Leaving an empty column unfilled for several turns is a legitimate strategy. The conditions that justify it:

No King improves your position. If all visible Kings have no accessible Queen of the right color, filling the column adds no immediate build potential.

You need the relay space. If there are several tableau cards you want to move in a specific order and no clean path exists, using the empty column as a temporary relay for one move unlocks a sequence of reveals that would otherwise be impossible.

You are early in the stock passes. In the first pass through the stock, you have the most information coming. Waiting a few turns before committing a King lets you see more of what is coming, which sometimes reveals a much better King candidate than what is currently available.

The rule of thumb: if you cannot name a specific advantage the King placement creates, wait.

## Matching King Color to Tableau Needs

Klondike builds alternate colors, so every King starts a sequence that requires a Queen of the opposite color. Before placing a King, look at which colors are available as Queens on the current tableau.

If two black Queens are visible and unplaced, a red King can absorb one immediately. If no Queen of either color is accessible, any King you place will sit idle.

Suit color also affects what comes next. A black King on an empty column will eventually need a red Queen, then a black Jack, and so on. If the red cards in those ranks are mostly buried or already placed elsewhere, the sequence will stall. That is not a reason to avoid the placement, but it is a reason to watch that column carefully and not over-invest in building it at the expense of other progress.

## Timing: Early, Mid, and Late Game Differences

King placement looks different depending on where you are in the game.

**Early game**: the board has many face-down cards and the stock is fresh. An empty column here is rare and valuable. Hold it as a relay space for at least a few moves before committing a King. The [opening moves guide](/blog/klondike-opening-moves) covers why early-game empty spaces are worth protecting.

**Mid-game**: face-down card count is dropping and the tableau is more visible. Now you have a clearer read on which suits need support and which Kings have useful sequences below them. This is usually the right time to commit a King to an empty column.

**Late game**: if an empty column appears with few face-down cards left, filling it with a King is usually correct. The board is readable enough that you know which King will contribute most to the remaining foundations.

You can [play Klondike with undo](/klondike) and practice deliberate King placements. After each game, ask whether your empty column King choices led to productive sequences or whether they stalled and needed to be rearranged later. That self-review builds pattern recognition faster than reading alone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I ever move a King out of an empty column once I've placed it there?

In standard Klondike, you can move a King back to an empty column, but only if another empty column exists for it to go to. In practice, this rarely comes up. The more useful question is whether you should have placed the King at all. If the sequence stalled, you are usually better off freeing the King by moving the Queen and lower cards elsewhere, then using the column as open space again.

### What if I only have one King and no visible Queens?

Fill the column with the King and start drawing from the stock. A Queen will come. The main cost of placing a King with no Queen is that you give up the relay space, so look for any rearrangements you can make before committing.

### Does the suit of the King matter beyond color?

Suit matters for in-suit builds and foundation progress, but in standard Klondike the most important factor is color (for alternating sequences). The practical difference between a King of Hearts and a King of Diamonds is mainly which Queens they can receive. Both are red, so a black Queen can land on either.

### How do I know if I picked the wrong King?

The clearest signal is a stalled sequence. If the King you placed has had the same face-up cards sitting on it for two or more stock passes without a new addition, you either picked the wrong King for the current state of the board or placed it too early. The [Klondike mid-game strategy guide](/blog/klondike-mid-game-strategy) covers how to handle sequences that stop growing.

### Is it ever wrong to open an empty column in the first place?

Yes. Some moves that clear the last card from a column create the empty space by moving a King or long sequence out of a useful position elsewhere. Before completing the clear, check whether the resulting empty column is worth more than whatever you gave up to create it. If the displaced sequence has nowhere useful to go, you may have traded a productive position for open space you cannot capitalize on yet.
