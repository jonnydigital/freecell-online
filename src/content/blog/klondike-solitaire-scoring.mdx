Most Klondike players watch the score go up without knowing what triggers each change. Understanding the scoring system will not make you win more games, but it will help you understand why certain moves are worth more than others and what the time bonus is actually rewarding.

## Key Takeaways

- Foundation moves score 10 points each. They are the highest-value individual moves in the game.
- Moving a card from the stock to the tableau scores 5 points.
- Moving a card from the foundation back to the tableau costs 15 points.
- Winning quickly earns a time bonus on top of your raw move score.

## The Basic Point Values

Klondike scoring rewards progress toward winning. The more you advance the game, the more points you accumulate. Here are the standard values used in most online implementations:

**Foundation moves: 10 points each.** Every time you send a card to one of the four foundation piles, you gain 10 points. Since you need to place 52 cards total to win, a clean win with no backward moves earns 520 points just from foundation placements.

**Stock to tableau moves: 5 points each.** When you draw a card from the stock and play it directly to a tableau column, you score 5 points. This only applies when you actually place the card on the tableau. Drawing and discarding to the waste pile scores nothing.

**Turning over a face-down card: 5 points.** Some versions award 5 points when you flip a face-down tableau card. This varies by implementation, so you may or may not see this in the version you are playing.

**Foundation back to tableau: -15 points.** If you move a card from the foundation back down to the tableau, you lose 15 points. This is intentional. The penalty discourages reversing progress, since a foundation card should generally stay there.

## The Time Bonus

When you win a game, most Klondike implementations add a time bonus on top of your move score. The faster you win, the larger the bonus.

The classic Windows calculation is roughly: bonus = 700,000 / seconds taken. That formula rewards finishing in under a few minutes with a large addition. A 10-minute win earns roughly 1,166 bonus points. A 3-minute win earns around 3,889.

The time bonus explains why two wins with identical moves can end with very different scores. A player who knows the position well and moves quickly will outscore a slower player who made the same choices.

In Draw 3 mode, the pace tends to involve more cycling, which can eat into time. The [Klondike Draw 1 vs Draw 3 guide](/blog/klondike-draw-1-vs-draw-3) covers how the two modes compare on difficulty and decision pacing.

## How Vegas Scoring Differs

The standard scoring described above is the default in most online Klondike games. Vegas scoring works on a completely different system: you pay a fixed stake per game and earn back money per card sent to the foundation.

In Vegas mode, you typically pay $52 to play and earn $5 per card that reaches the foundation. A full win returns $260 on a $52 stake, for a net gain of $208. Partial wins return less, and unwinnable deals cost you the whole stake.

Vegas scoring removes the time bonus and makes the financial math the focus. The [Klondike Vegas scoring guide](/blog/klondike-vegas-scoring-math) goes through the exact break-even math and when Vegas mode is worth playing for score rather than pure wins.

## What the Score Actually Tells You

The score is mostly a reflection of how much of the tableau you cleared, with a bonus for finishing fast. It does not measure efficiency or decision quality directly. A player who moves cards to the foundation 20 times and back once ends with fewer points than one who sent 18 cards up cleanly.

For beginners, the score should not drive your decisions. Moving a card to the foundation feels good because it adds 10 points, but premature foundation moves can block the game. ## Does the Score Reset Between Games?

This depends on the version. In classic implementations, each game has its own score and the score resets on a new deal. Some modern versions track a cumulative score across sessions.

If you are aiming for a high score on a specific deal, the key levers are: win cleanly with no backward foundation moves, win quickly to maximize the time bonus, and draw efficiently from the stock to stack up 5-point placements.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many points do you get for winning Klondike?

A clean win earns 520 base points from 52 foundation placements, plus a time bonus. The time bonus typically ranges from around 1,000 points for a slow win up to 5,000 or more for a fast one. The exact formula varies by implementation.

### Does the scoring affect gameplay decisions?

It should not. Your decisions should focus on winning the game, not maximizing score. The scoring rewards winning, so if you play to win, the score follows. The only exception is Vegas mode, where partial completions earn money back and the math changes which games are worth continuing.

### What happens to my score if I use undo?

In most implementations, undo does not directly subtract from your score. However, undoing a foundation move removes those 10 points, and you earn them again if you re-send the card. The -15 penalty applies when you move a card from the foundation to the tableau manually, not when you undo the foundation move.

### Is there a perfect Klondike score?

There is a practical maximum based on moves made. A complete win with no backward moves, fast completion, and efficient stock draws will produce the highest possible score for a given deal. The exact theoretical maximum depends on the version's time bonus formula and how it handles stock-to-tableau placements.

### Does the score carry over if I restart a game?

No. In standard play, each game has its own score and restarting gives you a fresh deal at zero. Cumulative scoring across games is a feature of some competitive implementations but is not standard in most free online versions.
