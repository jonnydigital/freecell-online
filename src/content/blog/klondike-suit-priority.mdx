Most Klondike guides tell you to send cards to the foundation as soon as you can. That advice is right most of the time, but it leaves out an important follow-up question: when you can build more than one suit at once, which one do you push first?

Suit priority is one of the quieter strategy concepts in Klondike. Getting it right does not guarantee a win. Getting it wrong can block your tableau without you noticing until several moves later.

## Key Takeaways

- Building all four suits at roughly the same speed gives you the most tableau flexibility through the middle game.
- Prioritize a suit when the cards that need to move off the tableau are all from that suit.
- Never rush one suit so far ahead of the others that the cards below it become stranded.
- Low cards (aces, 2s, 3s) are almost always safe to send up immediately.
- Check the [Klondike foundation timing guide](/blog/klondike-foundation-timing) before sending a 4, 5, or 6 that might still be useful in the tableau.

## Why Foundation Order Actually Matters

The foundation piles are not just the win condition. They also clear the tableau. Every card you send up is a card that stops blocking tableau columns. And once a blocking card moves, the cards below it become accessible.

That sounds simple. But here is the catch: moving a card to the foundation is one-way in standard Klondike. You cannot pull it back. So if you send a red 7 to the foundation before you have moved the black 8 off a buried pile, you may have eliminated the only card that could slide onto a red 9 elsewhere on the tableau.

Suit priority is about sequencing. It means thinking about which suits are most useful to clear from the tableau at any given moment, rather than just reaching for whatever is on top.

## Build All Four Suits at Similar Speeds by Default

The safest default is to advance all four suits at roughly the same rate. When you have an ace of spades and an ace of hearts available, move both to the foundation in a reasonable timeframe rather than racing one to the 5 while ignoring the other.

Balanced foundation building keeps your tableau flexible. Here is why: when one suit is at 6 and another is still at 2, you have a wide range of cards that cannot go to the foundation yet. A 5 or 6 of the lower suit is still stuck in the tableau and may be sitting on something useful. The longer it stays, the longer it blocks.

Even building also gives you more options for moving sequences. In Klondike, you place a red card on a black card one rank higher. That depends on both colors being available in similar ranges. A large gap between your red suits and your black suits narrows the number of legal tableau moves you can make.

Start each game by tracking which aces are visible and which are still in the stock. Suits that are furthest behind usually deserve attention first.

## When to Prioritize One Suit Over Another

There are three situations where you should push one suit ahead of the balanced default.

**The column-clearing priority.** If you need to flip face-down cards in a specific column, and the blocking face-up cards are all from the same suit, advancing that suit lets you clear them faster. For example, a column has face-up cards 6, 5, 4 of clubs sitting on top of four face-down cards. Sending those clubs to the foundation frees those hidden cards immediately. The payoff for rushing clubs is direct and fast.

**The buried ace.** When an ace is buried deep in a tall column, the suits that depend on it are going to lag behind regardless of what you do. You cannot build spades if the ace of spades is under eight face-down cards. In that case, push the suits whose aces are already in play. Make progress where you can.

**The end of the stock.** Once you have cycled through the stock in Draw 1, you have a fixed set of remaining options. Look at what cards are still on the tableau and what the foundations need. Prioritize whichever suit has the most cards currently accessible. The [Klondike endgame strategy guide](/klondike-endgame-strategy) covers this phase in detail.

## The Buried Low Card Problem

Buried aces get the most attention, but buried 2s and 3s cause the same problem. You can have an ace on the foundation and still be stuck if the 2 of that suit is under six face-down cards. The suit is frozen until you reach it.

Track not just the aces but also the 2s and 3s early in the game. These low cards are the most common foundation blockers. When you find a buried 2 in a tall column, that column becomes a dig priority. You need to reach it before you can make real progress on that suit.

Meanwhile, build the suits that are unblocked. Do not wait for symmetry when one suit is stalled. Use the suits in play and keep digging toward the ones that are not.

## Suit Priority in the Late Game

Once you are past mid-game and have turned over most of the face-down cards, the calculus shifts. At this point, one suit is often noticeably ahead of the others. That suit is worth pushing further. Getting it completed first gives you a significant board simplification.

Think of the first completed suit as a safety valve. Every card of that suit is gone from the tableau, which reduces interference for the remaining three suits. The other suits benefit because they have fewer cross-suit complications to navigate.

By the late game you also have better information. You can often see the remaining cards and plan a sequence of foundation moves. Use the [Klondike cheat sheet](/klondike-cheat-sheet) for quick reference on what each column needs.

## What Suit Priority Looks Like in Practice

Here is a simple process to apply at decision points during the game:

1. Find all visible aces and move them to the foundation.
2. Identify which suits have the next two or three cards accessible in the tableau.
3. Advance the suits where the next card is face-up and easy to reach.
4. Slow down a suit if sending the next card would remove a card needed to support other sequences.
5. Check whether any suit is falling far behind. If one is three or more ranks lower than the others, prioritize uncovering its lower cards.

This takes about ten seconds to run through at each major decision point. It is not a rigid algorithm but a set of checks you apply against the current board. [Play Klondike online](/klondike) to practice applying these checks in real games.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does it matter which suit I build first if I have a choice?

Yes, but only marginally in the early game. The bigger concern is not picking a single suit to favor, but watching for tableau blockers. The suit that lets you clear the most face-down cards is usually the right choice in the opening. By mid-game, suit balance matters more.

### Should I always send a card to the foundation as soon as I can?

Not always. A card that is still useful in the tableau for moving sequences should stay there until a better move is available. This is especially true for 4s, 5s, and 6s, which are commonly needed as intermediate tableau targets. Low cards like aces, 2s, and 3s are usually safe to send up immediately.

### What if I can only build one suit at a time because the others are buried?

Build the one you can. Do not wait for balance if the alternative is making no progress. Use the suit that is accessible, and keep digging toward the buried ones in the background by working those columns.

### Does suit priority change between Draw 1 and Draw 3?

In Draw 3, the stock is harder to cycle through, so tableau flexibility is more important. This often means being more conservative about sending tableau cards to the foundation before you have set up good sequences. In Draw 1, you cycle through the stock more cleanly and can plan further ahead. The [Draw 1 vs Draw 3 guide](/blog/klondike-draw-1-vs-draw-3) covers how each mode changes your overall approach.
