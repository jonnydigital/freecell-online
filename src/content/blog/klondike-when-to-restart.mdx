The urge to restart a Klondike game usually arrives around move 30 or 40, when the board looks stuck and the stock has been through twice with nothing useful. The game might not be over yet. But it might be.

That distinction matters more than most players realize. Restarting too early builds a habit of abandoning games the moment they get hard. Staying too long in a genuinely dead game wastes time on moves that cannot help you. The skill is reading which one you are facing.

## Key Takeaways

- Most Klondike games that feel unwinnable are not. Run a check before restarting.
- A game is genuinely over when the stock is exhausted with no progress and every face-down card is blocked beneath unplayable sequences.
- The [Klondike dead game detection guide](/blog/klondike-dead-game-detection) has a step-by-step test for confirming a loss.
- Restarting too often prevents you from learning what recovery looks like. Push through hard games when any viable path remains.
- Use [free Klondike with undo](/klondike) to replay difficult positions until you either solve them or confirm they are genuinely unwinnable.

## Why Restarting Too Early Is Costly

A stuck-looking board is usually a board that needs a specific sequence of moves you have not found yet. If you restart the moment the game gets hard, you never practice navigating those tight positions.

The players who improve fastest are the ones who finish games, even losing ones. A loss that required thirty more moves teaches you something. A restart teaches you nothing about how the board actually closes out.

Klondike has an estimated win rate of roughly 79 percent in Draw 1 and around 11 to 25 percent in Draw 3, depending on how you play. Those numbers assume players who finish their games. If you restart every time the win seems unlikely, your effective rate drops well below that floor. The [Klondike probability guide](/klondike-probability) explains the actual odds in more detail.

## Signs a Game Is Still Alive

Before deciding to restart, check for these indicators that the game is still recoverable.

**Face-down cards remain.** Every face-down card is a potential useful card. As long as face-down cards exist on the tableau, you have not seen the full board. A single buried card can be exactly the one you need.

**At least one useful stock card appeared on the last pass.** If the stock delivered one usable card on the last cycle, more may come once tableau changes create new placement options.

**A tableau rearrangement exists that you have not tried.** Sometimes a move you skipped earlier would change the stock access on the next cycle. The [thinking ahead guide](/blog/klondike-thinking-ahead) covers how to spot multi-step sequences that are easy to miss.

**A foundation card is close.** If a foundation pile is at 5 and the 6 is on the tableau but covered, that is a goal worth working toward, not abandoning.

## How to Know the Game Is Actually Over

A Klondike game is genuinely unwinnable when all of these are true at the same time:

1. The stock has cycled at least twice with no new plays possible.
2. Every face-down card on the tableau is inaccessible because the cards above it cannot be moved anywhere useful.
3. No foundation progress is possible without first uncovering a buried card, and no path to uncovering it exists.
4. No tableau rearrangement creates a new opening.

That is a specific set of conditions. If even one is false, the game might still have a line. The [dead game detection guide](/blog/klondike-dead-game-detection) walks through this test with concrete examples of what a truly closed position looks like versus one that just needs a different move order.

## The Two-Pass Rule

A simple filter that cuts out most premature restarts: do not restart until the stock has cycled at least twice without producing a single useful card.

The first time through the stock, you have seen maybe 60 to 70 percent of the deck. The second pass fills in a lot more. Only after two full unproductive passes is the stock likely to be a dead resource.

If the second pass still turns up something usable, go a third time. The board is still alive.

## What to Do Before the Restart

If you have done the full check and the game is genuinely lost, restart. But first, spend a minute reviewing the board as it stands.

Look for the moment things went wrong. Was there an opening move that locked out a suit? Did you place a King too early and waste an empty column? Did you send a card to the foundation too soon, blocking a tableau sequence that needed it?

The [opening moves guide](/blog/klondike-opening-moves) covers the early decisions that have the most downstream impact. Most dead games trace back to one of four or five specific early decisions, and recognizing those in hindsight helps you avoid them next time.

## In Draw 3 Specifically

Draw 3 Klondike has a much lower win rate than Draw 1, and positions can feel dead much earlier. But the same principle applies: a game is not over until all four conditions above are true simultaneously.

Draw 3 positions feel hard sooner because the offset structure of the stock limits which cards are accessible per pass. Cards that are three positions apart in the stock cycle are linked. If two of those three are blocked, the third often becomes inaccessible too.

The [Draw 3 cycle math guide](/blog/klondike-draw-3-cycle-math) explains the offset structure in detail. Understanding it helps you see that apparent dead ends in Draw 3 sometimes have a specific fix: one tableau change shifts the offset and brings a buried card back into reach.

## The Better Habit

Finish more games. Even losing ones. The finish teaches you the shape of a closed board, the sequences that run out, and the specific mistakes that created the problem.

Restart only after confirming the full checklist above. When you do restart, take a moment to review the last board state before dealing again. Each restart should produce a mental note about what to do differently, not just a fresh shuffle.

Over time, the number of hard games you rescue will increase. So will your ability to tell a recoverable position from a finished one, faster and with less guesswork.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many times should I cycle through the stock before restarting?

At minimum, two full passes with no new plays. If the second pass produced even one usable card, that changes what the stock might offer after a tableau adjustment. The two-pass rule filters out the majority of premature restarts.

### Is it always worth finishing a Draw 3 game that looks stuck?

Not always. Draw 3 games can reach confirmed dead positions after fewer cycles than Draw 1, because the offset structure locks more cards out per pass. But the confirmation checklist is the same: all face-down cards must be inaccessible, and the stock must be truly dry. If any face-down card could become reachable, the game is not over.

### Does using undo change whether I should restart?

Undo lets you reverse individual moves without restarting the whole game. If the game is recoverable and undo is available, using it to explore a different path is better than restarting. Reserve the restart for games where even unlimited undo cannot create a valid move sequence.

### What if I have been playing the same game for 20 minutes and it still is not over?

Length of time is not a signal that the game is unwinnable. It may mean you are stuck in a loop. Step back and check whether the same stock cards are cycling past unused. If they are, the issue is likely a blocked tableau rearrangement, not a dead game. The [Klondike endgame guide](/blog/klondike-solitaire-endgame) covers how to break out of these loops.

### How do I get better at knowing when to give up?

Play more games to their conclusion, even when they look lost. Each finished game gives you data on what a true dead end looks like. The more boards you see close out completely, the faster you will recognize one mid-game.
