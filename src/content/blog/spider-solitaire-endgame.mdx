The endgame in Spider Solitaire starts when the stock is empty and your suits are close to complete. At this point, the game is no longer about finding space to work. It is about finding the exact sequence of moves that gets each suit's King-through-Ace run sent to the foundation.

Most Spider players lose the endgame in one of three ways: completing suits in an inefficient order, spending empty columns on moves that do not lead to a completion, or stranding a card that another suit needed as a stepping stone.

## Key Takeaways

- The Spider endgame starts when the stock is empty and at least two suits are close to complete.
- Complete one suit at a time. Do not split focus across all suits when you are within range of finishing one.
- Empty columns in the endgame are routing tools, not storage. Reserve them for rerouting cards in nearly complete runs, not for parking mixed stacks.
- Suits that are one or two cards from complete should be finished before you start pulling apart other columns.
- The order your suits complete in matters. Build the order that leaves the most column space for the suits still in progress.

## When the Spider Endgame Starts

In a typical Spider game, the endgame begins once the stock is fully dealt. No new cards are coming. What you see is what you have.

In practice, you can recognize the endgame when:

- All five deals from the stock have been played.
- At least two of your eight suits have ten or more cards assembled in proper sequence.
- The board has two or more columns with nearly complete suited runs.

Before the stock empties, you still have the option of dealing new cards to shake up a stuck board. That option is gone in the endgame. From this point, you are solving a fixed puzzle with the cards in front of you.

## Completing Suits in the Right Order

Not all suits finish at the same cost. Some runs are already clean and only need one or two cards freed to send the full King-to-Ace sequence off. Others require rerouting multiple cards across columns before they are ready.

Start with the cheapest completion. If one suit only needs a King freed from the top of a stack, do that first. Finishing a cheap suit gives you an empty column. That empty column then funds the more expensive completions that follow.

The mistake is starting the most difficult suit first because it "needs the most work." Starting hard means spending your empty columns early. When you reach the easy suits later, you may not have the space to maneuver.

The guide on [Spider suit completion strategy](/blog/spider-solitaire-suit-completion) covers how to assess which suits are actually closest to done, since the count of cards in a run is not always the same as the number of moves needed to finish it.

## How to Use Empty Columns in the Endgame

Empty columns serve a different function in the endgame than in the mid-game. In the mid-game, empty columns absorb cards while you rearrange. In the endgame, empty columns are routing slots.

A routing slot lets you temporarily park part of a run while you extract a needed card from underneath it. You split the run, extract the card, reassemble from the other side, and the column clears again.

Used this way, one empty column can fund multiple completions as long as you plan the sequence before moving anything.

The trap is using the empty column to store a short mixed-suit stack out of the way. Once a mixed stack sits in an empty column, it cannot fund any completion without costing you another column to remove it later.

Review the [Spider empty columns guide](/blog/spider-solitaire-empty-columns) if you want a fuller breakdown of when to spend column space versus hold it.

## Rank Dependencies Between Suits

Players who rush through a visible Spider win often create a block just before the final suit completes. It happens because a card that looks ready to move has a hidden dependency: another suit needs it as a stepping stone a few moves ahead.

Before sending any card to the foundation in the endgame, check whether any other incomplete suit needs a card of the same rank. If a suit needs an 8 to extend its run, and you are about to send the only available 8 to the foundation for a different suit, hold it until the other suit can accept that 8 first.

The [Spider suit strategy page](/spider-suit-strategy) covers the full rule on rank dependencies between suits. In the endgame, this matters more than at any earlier stage because the stock is gone and you cannot deal new copies of a rank you sent away too early.

## Reading a Won Position

Once you can trace an unblocked path from the current board to all eight suits complete, you have won. The remaining work is executing the sequence.

Do not rush that execution. Take time to read the full path before touching a card. The only way to lose a position that is mathematically won is to make an ordering error mid-execution.

If you find a win path and have undo available, play it through and back up if you hit a block. [Play Spider free](/spider) to practice reading endgame positions without risk. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know when the Spider endgame has started?

The endgame begins when the stock is empty and no further deals are possible. At that point, every card on the board is fixed. You are working with what you have, and the task shifts from managing space to sequencing completions.

### Should I complete suits as fast as possible?

Complete them as efficiently as possible, which is not always the same as fastest. The most efficient order is the one that preserves column space for each successive completion. Finishing the cheapest suit first gives you an empty column that funds the next. Racing through the hardest suit first can leave you with no room to finish the easier ones.

### What is the biggest endgame mistake in Spider?

Using an empty column to store a mixed-suit stack you do not need to move right now. Storing a mixed stack in an empty column locks that space until you spend another column to deal with it later. Reserve empty columns for routing moves that directly advance a suit completion.

### Can I still lose a Spider game once I am in the endgame?

Yes. An endgame position is not automatically won. If your suits are incomplete and empty columns run out, the game can still lock. The risk goes down significantly once you have two or more suits completed, because each completion frees a column and that column funds the next one.
