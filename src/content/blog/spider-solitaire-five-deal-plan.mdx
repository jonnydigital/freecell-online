Spider Solitaire does not give you unlimited chances. It gives you exactly five deals of ten cards, and when those are gone, the board stays as it is until you either clear it or concede. Most players know this in theory but play as if the stock is a safety net. It is not.

Treating each deal as a stage gate, with specific goals to reach before you click the stock, changes how you think about the whole game.

## Key Takeaways

- Spider deals 54 cards to start the board, with 50 more in the stock across five deals of ten.
- Each deal buries whatever runs you built. Clear what you can before clicking.
- Before deal 1, aim for at least one or two in-suit connections and no empty columns.
- By deal 3, you should have at least one suit in progress. If not, the endgame will be very hard.
- The final deal is the last time the board gets harder. After that, every move is toward completion or stall.

## The Starting Board

The opening layout places 54 cards across ten columns, with only one face-up card per column visible. The remaining 44 are face-down and hidden until revealed.

This is the most information-scarce point in the game. You can see one card per column and nothing else. Your first moves work with very little data, which is why the [opening strategy guide](/spider-opening-strategy) focuses on building structure rather than chasing sequences.

Your opening goals before clicking the stock:
- Build any in-suit connections you can from the visible cards
- Flip a face-down card or two to gather more information
- Avoid creating positions you cannot recover from

If you reach the first deal with one partial in-suit run already started, that is a solid opening.

## Before Deal 1: Set Up the Board

By the time you are ready to click the stock for the first deal, you want:

**At least one face-down card flipped.** Each flip gives you more information about what is coming. Even one new card revealed before the deal changes what you can do with the incoming ten.

**No isolated mismatched sequences blocking your best columns.** The [opening strategy guide](/spider-opening-strategy) covers which columns to prioritize. Columns with more face-down cards underneath are worth more investment.

**Ideally, no empty columns.** Dealing into an empty column means one deal card lands there instead of on a column that needed it. That can be acceptable, but it costs you positioning. The [when-to-deal checklist](/blog/spider-solitaire-when-to-deal) has a mandatory pre-deal step: fill every empty column before clicking the stock.

## After Deal 1: Building In-Suit Foundations

Deal 1 adds ten cards, one on each column. The board is now more crowded than the opening but still readable. This is the best time to establish in-suit runs.

Aim for before deal 2:
- At least two or three in-suit sequences of two or more cards
- One empty column created, or close to it
- No column buried so deep you cannot see a path to its face-down cards

If you are playing 1-suit Spider, any K-through-low sequence of matching suit counts. In 2-suit and 4-suit, you are tracking suit-specific runs, which requires more attention to which suits have appeared where. The [suit strategy guide](/spider-suit-strategy) covers how to prioritize across variants.

## After Deal 2: First Suit in Sight

Deal 2 is roughly the midpoint of the dealing phase. After this deal, you have more cards on the board than you started with, and three deals remain.

Aim for before deal 3:
- One suit that is clearly in progress and within reach of completion
- Two or more empty columns created at some point (even if not currently empty)
- No column with more than three buried face-down cards you cannot access

The [mid-game guide](/blog/spider-solitaire-mid-game) covers how to manage board density at this stage. The key failure mode after deal 2 is a board where every column is capped by mismatched sequences that block buried face-down cards. If you are heading into deal 3 with that board state, the game may already be decided.

## After Deal 3: Point of No Return

Deal 3 is where close games often separate from lost ones. After deal 3, two deals remain. The board is getting dense.

Before deal 4, you need:
- At least one suit either complete or one or two cards away from completion
- Empty columns available to maneuver
- Some face-down cards still accessible

If no suit is near completion by this point, deal 4 will bury the progress you need to make. The [endgame strategy guide](/spider-endgame-strategy) explains what the closing phase requires. The setup for it has to exist before deal 3 lands.

This is also the moment to make an honest assessment of whether the game is still winnable. The [when to restart guide](/blog/spider-solitaire-when-to-restart) covers how to tell a genuinely lost game from one that is simply hard.

## After Deal 4: Last Preparation Before the End

Deal 4 is your last chance to adjust the board before the final deal. After this, 94 of 104 cards are on the table.

Before deal 5:
- Complete any near-finished suits you can
- Secure empty columns as maneuvering space
- Avoid burying useful sequences under cards you cannot move later

The final deal will land on whatever state you created here. If your best in-suit runs are exposed, they will get buried. That is unavoidable. The goal is to minimize how much damage the last deal does by leaving as many completion paths intact as possible.

## After Deal 5: No More Cards

The final deal is the last new information. After it, all 104 cards are on the board and no more draws are coming. The game is now pure execution.

If you have suits within reach and enough empty column space to maneuver, the game is winnable. If not, work through the remaining plays until a confirmed dead position appears.

The [Spider Solitaire cheat sheet](/spider-cheat-sheet) has a quick reference for suit completion requirements and minimum empty column needs in the endgame.

## Why Most Players Lose at Deal 3 or 4

The majority of lost Spider games stall not at deal 5 but at deal 3 or 4, when empty column space runs out before any suit is complete. Without an empty column, you cannot rearrange sequences long enough to close out a suit. Without closing a suit, you never recover the freed column.

The fix is earlier suit focus. Players who win consistently pick one suit by deal 2 and push it toward completion before dealing again. They sacrifice some board flexibility to guarantee one completion path before the board gets too crowded.

If you find yourself arriving at deal 4 with no suit near complete and no empty columns, the [Spider Solitaire stuck guide](/blog/spider-solitaire-stuck) covers what recovery options still exist and when the game is genuinely over.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many total cards does Spider Solitaire use?

Spider Solitaire uses two full decks, which is 104 cards. The initial layout deals 54 to the board. The remaining 50 are split across five deals of ten.

### Do I have to use all five deals?

No. If you clear suits fast enough, you can win without dealing all five. In practice, most games require three or four deals at minimum. Using fewer deals is harder but more efficient when you can manage it.

### What if I have an empty column before a deal?

Fill the empty column before dealing. That is a firm rule from the [when-to-deal checklist](/blog/spider-solitaire-when-to-deal). An empty column before a deal should be filled deliberately with a card that sets up a sequence, not filled at random just before clicking the stock.

### Can I win a 4-suit game if no suit is complete by deal 3?

It is possible but unlikely. Without a complete suit and its freed column by deal 3, the board density in deals 4 and 5 makes maneuvering very difficult. The [4-suit strategy guide](/blog/spider-solitaire-4-suit-strategy) covers the specific adjustments needed in harder variants.

### What is the most common reason 4-suit Spider games end without a win?

Running out of empty columns in the mid-game is the most common cause. Without empty columns, you cannot rearrange sequences to complete a suit. Protecting empty column space from deal 2 onward is the core mid-game skill in 4-suit play.
