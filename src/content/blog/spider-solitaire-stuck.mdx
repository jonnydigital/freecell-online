The feeling of being stuck in Spider Solitaire is common. You scan the tableau, nothing obvious stands out, and the temptation is to deal from the stock or undo back several moves. Before doing either, there is usually more to check.

A stuck-looking position is not the same as a dead position. Dead positions cannot be won regardless of what you do. Stuck-looking positions often have moves you have not found yet, or a dealing choice that resets your options. The difference matters because the right response to each is different.

## Key Takeaways

- Most positions that feel stuck have at least one unexplored move. Do a full tableau scan before dealing.
- Empty columns are your most flexible resource. Use them for temporary moves before concluding there's nothing left to do.
- Dealing from the stock when the tableau is not ready often converts a stuck position into a losing one.
- A position is genuinely dead when no sequence can be completed, no empty column remains, and no useful deal is possible.

## Do a Full Tableau Scan First

The most common source of stuck positions is an incomplete scan. Players fix their attention on the top two or three columns, miss an available move in a column they haven't looked at recently, and conclude there is nothing to do.

Before treating a position as stuck, check every column from left to right for:

- Any card that can move onto a valid sequence, including partial sequences in other columns
- Any in-suit connection you haven't tried (in Spider, an in-suit move is always preferred over an out-of-suit one)
- Any sequence that can consolidate onto a longer run somewhere else

This sounds obvious but takes about twenty seconds to do properly. Most players spend ten seconds then move on. The extra ten seconds finds the move about a third of the time in genuinely stuck-looking positions.

The [Spider Solitaire position reading guide](/blog/spider-solitaire-position-reading) covers how to assess a board systematically rather than scanning by instinct. Applying that framework to a position you think is exhausted often reveals an option you skipped.

## Use Empty Columns Before Concluding You're Stuck

If you have an empty column, you are not yet stuck in any meaningful sense. An empty column can hold a sequence temporarily while you rearrange cards in other columns, which opens moves that aren't visible in the current layout.

The pattern: move a short sequence into the empty column, play the card now accessible in the column you just cleared, then move the sequence back to a better home. This costs moves but unlocks the card you needed.

Many players avoid this because it feels like wasted effort. It isn't. Moving a sequence to an empty column and back is a valid tactical operation. In 4-suit Spider, it is often the only way to make progress on a blocked board.

The [empty columns guide](/blog/spider-solitaire-empty-columns) covers how to value empty space and when to prioritize keeping a column open versus filling it.

## What Dealing Early Does to a Stuck Position

Dealing from the stock when the tableau is not ready is the most common mistake in stuck positions. It feels like a reset but it usually makes things worse.

A stock deal places 10 new cards, one on each column. If the columns already have blocked sequences, those 10 cards land on top of the blocks. The new cards may give you one or two new moves immediately, but the underlying structure gets more complex.

The [Spider dealing guide](/blog/spider-solitaire-when-to-deal) explains what the board needs before a deal is likely to help: ideally one or more near-complete in-suit sequences, at least one empty column or a clear path to one, and no buried high-rank cards trapped under mixed sequences.

If those conditions are not present, dealing makes the board harder, not easier. The stuck feeling after a premature deal is worse because the new cards add depth to the problem rather than resolving it.

## In-Suit vs. Out-of-Suit Moves When Options Are Limited

When options are genuinely scarce, the bias toward in-suit moves becomes critical. An in-suit move creates a sequence you can complete and remove from the board. An out-of-suit move creates a mixed sequence that looks playable but cannot be completed as-is.

In a stuck position, out-of-suit moves often feel like progress because they shift cards around. They are not progress unless they uncover a useful card or create access to an empty column.

Before making an out-of-suit move in a stuck position, ask: does this uncover a face-down card? Does it bring me closer to completing an in-suit sequence I'm partway through? If neither, the move may be postponing the problem.

At 4-suit difficulty, in-suit connections are rare enough that any in-suit move in a stuck position is almost always the correct play. At 1-suit and 2-suit, the abundance of in-suit moves means you should still find one before resorting to out-of-suit plays. The [Spider suit strategy guide](/blog/spider-solitaire-suit-strategy) covers why in-suit sequencing is the foundation of all difficulty levels.

## When a Position Is Actually Dead

Spider Solitaire has genuine dead positions: states from which no win is achievable regardless of play. Recognizing them early saves time.

The clearest signal: no in-suit moves are possible, all empty columns are filled, and the remaining stock deals will land on top of incomplete mixed sequences without creating new in-suit paths. At that point, the game is mathematically over.

Less obvious dead positions exist too. A board where all 10s are buried under sequences you cannot complete in the remaining deals is functionally dead, even if moves technically exist. The [Spider dead positions guide](/blog/spider-solitaire-dead-positions) maps the structures that create unrecoverable states so you can recognize them without playing through to the end.

A position that is stuck but not dead will have at least one of: an in-suit connection available, an empty column for tactical routing, or a stock deal remaining with a board ready to absorb it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if my Spider Solitaire game is really over?

If no in-suit sequences can be completed, all empty columns are filled, and no stock deals remain that could change the situation, the game is lost. If any of those conditions is not met, there is still something to try.

### Should I undo when I feel stuck in Spider Solitaire?

Undo is most useful for mechanical errors. If you feel stuck because the position is genuinely complex, undoing rarely helps unless you can identify exactly which specific move started the problem. Random undoing usually cycles through the same options without changing the outcome.

### When is it right to deal early in Spider Solitaire?

When you have at least one empty column, at least one in-suit sequence close to completion, and no face-down cards you cannot reach. Outside of those conditions, dealing early tends to add complexity rather than resolve it.

### What's the fastest way to check if any moves exist?

Run through the top card of each column against the top card of every other column. Look for both in-suit and mixed-suit connections. Then check whether any partial sequences can move as a group. This takes under a minute and is more reliable than scanning by instinct.

### Is it possible to get stuck in Spider 1-suit?

Yes, though it is less common because in-suit connections are easier to find. Most stuck positions in 1-suit come from filling all empty columns too early or dealing before enough in-suit sequences are near completion.
